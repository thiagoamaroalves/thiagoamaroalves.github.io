Prototype · Priority-aware load shedding

Priority under pressure.

A small, interactive model for exploring when an API should limit routes during high resource use or a route-specific p95 breach.

Question to exploreCan a service protect its most important routes by shedding lower-priority traffic when utilization is high or a route's p95 latency exceeds its budget?

Model assumptionsRoute names, p95 budgets, and latency values are illustrative. The 80% load trigger comes from the scenario described. Nothing here measures a real service.

05 · What the model predicts

Starting observations.

These are simulated outcomes, not production measurements.

80% load, p95 healthy

The deferrable route is limited first; essential and standard routes remain admitted.

65% load, essential p95 over budget

With the protection policy selected, lower-priority routes are limited even though utilization is below 80%.

65% load, deferrable p95 over budget

The deferrable route is limited on its own; healthy higher-priority routes remain admitted.

95% load

Both the deferrable and standard routes are limited; the essential route remains admitted.

Prototype only. This single file keeps state in memory and uses made-up inputs to test a decision rule. It does not emulate a server, establish a safe production threshold, or prove that shedding one route improves another route's latency. See the model notes for assumptions and open questions.
